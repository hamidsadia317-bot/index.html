Conceptual Foundations

Definition of Mizaj (Avicenna Canon)

Avicenna (Ibn Sina) Canon of Medicine

"Elements are divided into minute particles and when the qualities of these particles act and react with one another then a new pattern of qualities emerges, which are evenly distributed throughout the particles of all elements. Hence, that pattern of uniformity is called Mizaj / Temperament."

When the four primary elements (Air, Water, Fire, Earth) interact through their active/passive qualities (Heat, Cold, Moisture, Dryness), a state of equilibrium or specific pattern of uniformity develops to construct every physical matter and human humour.

Source: Canon of Medicine / Tibb-e-Nabwi Equilibrium & Pattern Uniformity

Systemic Taxonomy

Classification of Mizaj & Causes of Imbalance

Mizaj-e-Muatadil

Balanced Temperament

1. Absolute Balanced (Muatadil Haqeqi)

Theoretical state where four elements are quantitatively equal in power. Theoretical ideal.

2. Physical Balanced (Muatadil Tabbi)

Qualities are not strictly equal, but balanced according to nature and functional requirements across 4 patterns:

  • Species (Nui): Humans possess the overall most balanced temperament.
  • Race (Sinfi): Geographically adapted (e.g., Pakistani vs. Japanese climate patterns).
  • Individual (Shakhsi): Unique physiological baseline for each individual person.
  • Organ (Uzvi): Organ-specific balance (e.g., Bones are dry; Brain is moist).
Mizaj-e-Ghair Muatadil

Imbalanced Temperament

A. Simple / Single (Ghair Muatadil Sada)

Dominance of a single active (heat/cold) or passive (dryness/moistness) quality. Short-lived as simple imbalance quickly converts into compound states (e.g. excessive heat leads to dryness).

B. Compound (Ghair Muatadil Murakab)

Dual quality dominance: Hot & Dry, Cold & Dry, Hot & Wet, or Cold & Wet.

Pathological Etiology of Imbalance
1. Without Morbid Matter (Sua'e Mizaj Sada): Environmental causes (e.g. walking in snow).
2. With Morbid Matter (Sua'e Mizaj Madi): Infiltration into organ tissue or accumulation in excretory channels.

Drug Pharmacology & Four Physical Orders

Avicenna's Principle of Balanced / Normal Natural Drugs

A "balanced" drug does not mean its physical composition is identical to human tissue. Rather, when administered in prescribed doses, it interacts with the organism's vital force (Hararat-e-Ghariziyah) to elicit active constituents that maintain the individual's temperamental limits.

1st Order Drugs

Dose-related effect only. Safe for repeated administration without toxicity.

2nd Order Drugs

Potent active pharmacological action. Requires prescription supervision.

3rd & 4th Order Drugs

Toxic/Poisonous. Administered only in life-threatening emergencies with corrective agents (Corrigents).

Lifespan Trajectory

Temperament According to Age (Galen Theory)

Birth to 30 Yrs

San-e-Nmo (Growth)

Heat & Moistness Dominant

Quantity of heat is greater than quality; moistness is strong. Water, Fire, and Air elements dominate growth.

Clinical Association: Infant moisture → Diarrhea, vomiting; Teen heat → Inflammatory disorders.
30 to 40 Yrs

San-e-Waqoof (Maturity)

Moderate / Dryness Inclined

Peak human physiological powers. Fire and Soil elements in balanced moderation.

Clinical Association: Peak metabolic homeostasis & structural stability.
40 to 60 Yrs

San-e-Kaholet (Middle Age)

Coldness & Dryness Dominant

Human vitality remains strong, but soil element begins dominance as heat wanes.

Clinical Association: Onset of joint stiffness and metabolic slowdown.
60+ Yrs to Death

San-e-Shayokh (Senility)

Severe Cold & Dryness

Dryness markedly exceeds coldness. Soil element dominant; vital faculties decline.

Clinical Association: Osteoporosis, tissue desiccation, arterial stiffness.
